# BCS classification

The Biopharmaceutics Classification System (BCS) divides drug substances into four classes based on aqueous solubility and intestinal permeability:

| Class | Solubility | Permeability | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | High | High | Dissolution unlikely to limit absorption; biowaivers feasible |
| II | Low | High | Dissolution is the rate-limiting step; formulation matters |
| III | High | Low | Permeability is rate-limiting; biowaivers possible under FDA/EMA guidance |
| IV | Low | Low | Both rate-limiting; high formulation challenge |

**Solubility threshold (FDA):** A drug is "highly soluble" if its highest single therapeutic dose dissolves in ≤250 mL of aqueous media across pH 1.2–6.8 at 37 °C.

**Permeability threshold (FDA):** A drug is "highly permeable" if ≥85% of an administered oral dose is absorbed (or in vitro Caco-2 permeability comparable to internal standards of known high-permeability drugs).

**Biowaiver implications:**
- Class I drugs in IR oral solid forms may qualify for a *full BCS-based biowaiver* with comparative dissolution alone (FDA, EMA, WHO).
- Class III drugs may qualify for BCS-based biowaivers under FDA/EMA guidance with specific formulation constraints.
- Class II and IV drugs require in vivo BE studies for generic approval, with comparative dissolution as a supporting (not replacement) tool.
