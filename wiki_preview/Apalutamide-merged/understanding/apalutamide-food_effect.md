# Understanding — apalutamide food effect

The apalutamide tablet has a small, statistically distinguishable food effect: a high-fat meal reduces Cmax by ~16% (GMR 0.84, 90% CI 0.75–0.94) and delays tmax by about 2 hours, with AUC essentially unchanged ([[evidence/food_effect]]). The FDA reviewer concluded "no clinically relevant food effect" given the modest magnitude, the unchanged total exposure (AUC GMR 0.95–0.97), and the flat exposure-MFS relationship from the Phase 3 dataset ([[evidence/pkpd_model]] / [[studies/clinical/nct01946204]]). ERLEADA may therefore be administered with or without food.

The capsule formulation showed a similar pattern in a steady-state CRPC patient sub-study (Study 001, 2-way crossover, N=12 at Cycle 3): Cmax GMR 0.86 (0.76–0.96), AUC0-24 GMR 0.95 (0.89–1.02). N-desmethyl apalutamide showed even smaller food effects in both formulations (Cmax/AUC GMRs within 0.95–1.02).

Mechanistically, the modest food effect is consistent with apalutamide's BCS Class 2 properties ([[bcs-classification]]): a poorly water-soluble lipophilic drug whose absorption can be slightly delayed by gastric retention with food but is not strongly enhanced by bile-acid-stimulated solubilization (because the to-be-marketed tablet appears to use a solid-dispersion approach with HPMC-AS — see [[evidence/formulation]] — that already maintains apparent solubility in the GI lumen).
