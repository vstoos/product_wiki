# Understanding — apalutamide drug interactions (clinical)

## Apalutamide as a victim

Apalutamide is metabolized by CYP2C8 and CYP3A4, but the impact of perpetrators is modest at the therapeutic single-dose level ([[evidence/ddi_clinical]]). Itraconazole (strong CYP3A4 inhibitor) reduced Cmax by 22% with no AUC change. Gemfibrozil (strong CYP2C8 inhibitor) reduced Cmax by 21% but increased AUC0-672 by 53% and AUCinf by 68% — the largest documented victim DDI. At steady-state 240 mg QD, PBPK predicts strong CYP3A4 inhibition (ketoconazole) raises apalutamide Cmax/AUC by ~40–50% and strong CYP3A4/CYP2C8 induction (rifampin) lowers them by ~25–35%. The label does not require initial dose adjustment for any perpetrator but recommends considering a dose reduction based on tolerability when strong inhibitors are coadministered.

## Apalutamide as a perpetrator

Apalutamide is a strong CYP3A4 and CYP2C19 inducer, a weak CYP2C9 inducer in vivo, and a clinically meaningful inhibitor of multiple transporters ([[evidence/ddi_clinical]] / [[evidence/ddi_invitro]]). The induction effects are large:

- Midazolam (CYP3A4 substrate) AUC −92%
- Omeprazole (CYP2C19 substrate) AUC −85%
- S-warfarin (CYP2C9 substrate) AUC −46%
- Fexofenadine (P-gp substrate) AUC −30%
- Rosuvastatin (BCRP/OATP1B1 substrate) AUC −41%

Clinical management focuses on substitution where possible. For warfarin, INR monitoring is required at initiation and end of apalutamide therapy. The CYP2C8 substrate pioglitazone showed only an 18% AUC reduction — no dose adjustment.

## Acid-lowering agents

Because apalutamide is non-ionizable at physiological pH ([[evidence/physicochemical]]) and is delivered in a solid-dispersion formulation that maintains apparent solubility independent of luminal pH ([[evidence/formulation]]), proton pump inhibitors, H2-receptor antagonists, and antacids are not expected to affect apalutamide bioavailability.
