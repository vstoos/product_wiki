# Understanding — apalutamide BCS classification

## Class assignment

Apalutamide is a **BCS Class 2** compound: low solubility, high permeability.

- **Low aqueous solubility** is supported by the drug substance characterization: practically insoluble in aqueous media over a wide pH range, with the drug substance only stable up to pH 11 ([[evidence/solubility]] / [[evidence/physicochemical]]). The pKa of 9.7 (acidic carboxamide) puts the molecule in its protonated (less polar) form across the entire physiological pH range, so the dissolution-driven absorption ceiling is set by the formulation, not by the GI environment.
- **High permeability** is supported by absolute oral bioavailability ≈100% ([[evidence/pharmacokinetics]]), demonstrating that essentially all drug presented to the absorption window crosses the intestinal epithelium. PBPK considers apalutamide a P-gp substrate in vitro, but P-gp-mediated efflux is not rate-limiting at the 240 mg clinical dose ([[evidence/pharmacokinetics]]).

## Formulation strategy consequence

The Class 2 assignment explains the formulation choice: the to-be-marketed tablet uses HPMC-AS (hydroxypropyl methylcellulose-acetate succinate, an enteric pH-responsive polymer commonly used as a solid-dispersion carrier) alongside a wet granulation / direct compression matrix ([[evidence/formulation]]). The solid-dispersion approach maintains apparent solubility in the GI lumen, which (a) gives the high-permeability molecule something to absorb and (b) makes the absorption profile insensitive to gastric pH (hence the no-PPI-interaction conclusion in [[evidence/ddi_clinical]]).

## QC dissolution method consistent with Class 2

The dissolution method ([[evidence/dissolution_method]]) — USP App II at 75 rpm in 0.25% SLS (a surfactant) at pH 4.5 — is the kind of medium needed to discriminate solid-dispersion-stabilized release of a low-solubility compound: SLS provides micellar solubilization that mimics endogenous bile-acid-stabilized solubility in the small intestine.
