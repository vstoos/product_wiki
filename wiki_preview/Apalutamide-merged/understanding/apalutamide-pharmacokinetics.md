# Understanding — apalutamide pharmacokinetics

## Absorption and distribution

Apalutamide is absorbed quickly (Tmax ≈ 2 h, range 1–5) with absolute oral bioavailability near 100% ([[evidence/pharmacokinetics]]). Distribution is moderate (Vss/F ≈ 276 L), plasma protein binding is high (96%), and the blood-to-plasma ratio (~0.79) indicates predominantly plasma-compartment distribution. The drug substance is non-ionizable at physiological pH ([[evidence/physicochemical]]), which is why acid-lowering agents do not affect bioavailability.

## Metabolism and the autoinduction story

Metabolism is the main route of elimination. Apalutamide is metabolized to N-desmethyl apalutamide primarily by CYP2C8 (58% single-dose contribution) and CYP3A4 (13% single-dose contribution). At steady state under 240 mg QD, those contributions shift to 40% / 37% because apalutamide is itself a strong CYP3A4 inducer ([[evidence/ddi_clinical]] / [[evidence/ddi_invitro]]). Carboxylesterase also contributes. The effective steady-state half-life is about three days, and the active N-desmethyl metabolite accumulates 122-fold — making N-desmethyl-apalutamide a quantitatively important contributor to clinical effect.

## Variability profile

In healthy volunteers (parallel-group single-dose, Study 1011) the *total* CV% on Cmax and AUC is modest (~18–20%). In CRPC patients at 240 mg QD steady state (population PK), *total* CV% is higher (28% Cmax, 32% AUC0-24,ss for apalutamide; 18% Cmax, 19% AUC0-24,ss for the more buffered N-desmethyl) ([[evidence/pharmacokinetics]]). The healthy-volunteer-vs-patient gap reflects both biological heterogeneity (age, comorbidity, comedications) and dosing-stage variability (timing of intake, adherence). See [[understanding/apalutamide-pk-variability]] for inter- vs intra-subject decomposition.

## Exposure–response

No statistically significant exposure-MFS relationship was found in SPARTAN at 240 mg QD ([[evidence/pkpd_model]] / [[studies/clinical/nct01946204]]). Exposure-safety relationships do exist — higher exposure tracked with fatigue, rash, fall, and arthralgia — and dose reductions to 180 mg or 120 mg QD per protocol restored tolerability without efficacy loss in the modeled scenarios.

## QT/QTc

Apalutamide produces a modest, concentration-dependent QTcF prolongation (max mean ΔQTcF 12.4 ms; 90% upper CI 16.1 ms) at the therapeutic dose ([[studies/clinical/nct02578797]]). This drives the labeling caveats around electrolyte correction and avoidance in congenital long-QT.
