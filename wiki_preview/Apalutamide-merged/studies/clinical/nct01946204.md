# SPARTAN — apalutamide Phase 3 in NM-CRPC

## Identifiers

- **Canonical id:** `nct01946204`
- **NCT:** NCT01946204
- **Sponsor code:** ARN-509-003
- **Nickname:** SPARTAN (Selective Prostate AR Targeting with ARN-509)

## Overview

SPARTAN was a multinational, randomized (2:1), double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 study comparing apalutamide 240 mg QD orally vs placebo, administered with concurrent GnRH analogue or bilateral orchiectomy, in **1207 patients** with high-risk non-metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (NM-CRPC), defined as PSA doubling time ≤10 months. Primary endpoint: metastasis-free survival (MFS). Conducted at 332 centers in 26 countries.

## Data by source

### From NDA 210951 Multidisciplinary Review

- **Trial design:** Multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3.
- **Treatment:** Apalutamide 240 mg orally daily vs placebo.
- **Population:** High-risk NM-CRPC, PSADT ≤10 months, on continuous ADT with testosterone <50 ng/dL.
- **n_randomized:** 1207.
- **Primary endpoint:** Metastasis-Free Survival (MFS) by BICR.
- **Stratification factors:** PSADT (>6 vs ≤6 months); bone-sparing agent use (yes/no); locoregional disease (N0 vs N1).
- **Centers / countries:** 332 / 26.
- **Median treatment duration:** 16.9 months apalutamide arm vs 11.2 months placebo arm.
- **Key efficacy result (MFS):** Hazard ratio 0.280 (95% CI 0.227, 0.346) — apalutamide vs placebo.

## Safety highlights

- **AEs leading to discontinuation:** Apalutamide arm 11%, placebo arm 7.0%.
- **AEs leading to dose reduction:** Apalutamide arm 11%, placebo arm 3.3%.
- **Skin rash:** 24% apalutamide (191/803) vs 5.5% placebo (22/399); most Grade 1–2; resolved in ~2 months in 81%; 19 (2.4%) apalutamide patients discontinued for rash.
- **Seizure:** 0.2% apalutamide.

## Final overall-survival analysis (SUPPL_004 PMC fulfillment, 2020)

The FDA approved label update from final SPARTAN OS analysis was approved via NDA 210951/S-004 in 2020 (PMC fulfillment).

- **Final OS hazard ratio:** **0.78** (95% CI 0.64, 0.96), p=0.0161.
  - [[sources/suppl-004-210951s004lbl-hybrid|FDA Supplement S-004 Labeling]] [NDA-210951-S004-LBL | Labeling | Clinical Studies - SPARTAN OS Table | p.19]
  - Verbatim: "Hazard Ratio (95% CI) | 0.78 (0.64, 0.96) | p-valuea | 0.0161"
- **Median OS:** **73.9 months** apalutamide arm vs **59.9 months** placebo arm.
  - [[sources/suppl-004-210951s004lbl-hybrid|FDA Supplement S-004 Labeling]] [NDA-210951-S004-LBL | Labeling | Clinical Studies - SPARTAN OS Table | p.19]
  - Verbatim: "73.9 (61, NE) | 59.9 (53, NE)"
- **Time to cytotoxic chemotherapy:** **HR 0.63** (95% CI 0.49, 0.81), p=0.0002 — statistically significant delay with apalutamide.
  - [[sources/suppl-004-210951s004lbl-hybrid|FDA Supplement S-004 Labeling]] [NDA-210951-S004-LBL | Labeling | Clinical Studies - SPARTAN | p.20]
  - Verbatim: "Treatment with ERLEADA resulted in a statistically significant delay in the initiation of cytotoxic chemotherapy [HR = 0.63 (95% CI:0.49, 0.81), p=0.0002]"

## Japanese subgroup analysis (PMDA review)

SPARTAN enrolled Japanese patients as part of the multinational protocol; PMDA's review extracts the Japanese-only subgroup MFS analysis.

| Treatment arm | n | MFS events | Median MFS [95% CI] (months) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apalutamide | 34 | 5 (14.7%) | Not estimable [10.97, NE] |
| Placebo | 21 | 8 (38.1%) | 18.23 [11.04, 18.50] |

- **Japanese subgroup MFS hazard ratio (non-stratified Cox):** 0.565 (95% CI 0.181, 1.766), p=0.3207
  - [[sources/pmda-review-report-erleada-apalutamide-hybrid|PMDA Review Report]] [PMDA-RR-Erleada | Review Report | Section 7.1.3.1 Study 003 Japanese subgroup Table 31 | p.54]
  - Verbatim: "Number of patients | 34 | 21 ... Number of events (%) | 5 (14.7) | 8 (38.1) ... Hazard ratio [95% CI] | 0.565 [0.181, 1.766]"
- **PMDA interpretation:** The Japanese-subgroup HR did not reach statistical significance because of the small N (55 Japanese patients, 13 events). However, the **direction and magnitude (point estimate 0.565)** are consistent with the global ITT result (HR 0.280). PMDA concluded: "no clearly different trends were observed between the above results of the Japanese population and the entire population."
